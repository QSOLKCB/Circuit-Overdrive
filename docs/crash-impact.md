# Crash Lab impact authority

Rules 1 advance a flat X/Z test pad at 60 Hz. The immutable VANILLAJS arcade recipe supplies the 1,200 kg chassis, 1,750 kg·m² yaw inertia and 4.4 × 1.95 m dimensions. The game integrates translation and yaw. Three overlapping discs of radius 0.975 m form the advertised scalloped chassis envelope. Their centres sit at longitudinal stations −1.225, 0 and +1.225 m. Fixed test fixtures are finite line segments with rounded ends and a stated radius. Scenery outside the pad is decorative. Vertical flight, rollover, soft bodies, injury and structural safety analysis are outside this game model.

At each remaining interval the solver rotates each disc centre through its true arc. The current closest-point normal defines a separating plane. For remaining time h, projected relative contact-centre closing speed times h plus ½ (|stationA| ωA² + |stationB| ωB²) h² bounds closure on this plane. The positive root gives a safe advance for translating and rotating tips. This second-order bound lets separating tips slide alongside a rail. The witness tolerance is 10 nanometres. Earliest time wins, with numeric car ID, partner ID and fore/middle/aft feature order breaking exact ties. Time advances only to the verified pose. There are at most 40 distance advances per feature, 24 resolution passes per tick, four moving cars and eight fixtures. An exhausted budget holds the residual motion and reports the unadvanced seconds and measured residual penetration across all pairs. An impossible overlapping fixture layout ends the experiment for inspection and reset. Initial overlaps receive at most 0.1 m of positional correction per pass, shared by two movable cars. Penetration deeper than 2 mm is positional recovery. A separating witness receives zero impulse.

For normal **n** pointing from B to A, contact velocity on a car is **v** + ω × **r**. The normal effective inverse mass is the sum of 1/m + ((r × n)y)²/Iy for the moving participants. Fixed bodies contribute zero. At closing speed c above 10⁻⁷ m/s, Jn = (1 + e)c / inverseMass. Restitution is 0.14 on entry and zero in continuing contact. The tangent is (−nz,nx). Its impulse cancels post-normal tangential slip up to 0.24 Jn, using the tangent effective mass. Both impulses change translation and yaw, with an equal opposite impulse on the other car. A 2 mm clearance stabilizes the next sweep.

The contact receipt identifies the source stamp (null in an unstamped checkout), rules, tick, pair and ordinal. It preserves the point, normal, relative point velocity and speed, closing speed in m/s, effective mass in kg and both signed-axis impulses in N·s. Normal energy is ½ m_effective c². Dissipation is the before/after difference of total planar kinetic energy of the participating moving bodies, including yaw, across these impulses only. Both measures use joules. Drivetrain, tyres and positional corrections are outside this measurement.

Every applied impulse has a receipt. Only the first impulse in a contact episode qualifies as a new impact. The latch releases when the entire pair has more than 35 mm clearance at a solver observation. Repeated support impulses retain their measurements and cannot award another impact. Local X points right and local −Z points forward. The larger fraction of chassis half-width or half-length at the surface witness selects the struck face. Exact diagonal ties use the longitudinal face. Reproduction comparisons use exact JSON data in the same JS runtime and numerical tolerances for analytic tests.

Touring and Nitro retain the horizontal swept-footprint solver in `src/contact.js`. Rocket retains its 3D chassis, rail and deck constraints in `src/rocket-contact.js`. Neither imports the laboratory authority.
